# Preface

There is no shortage of open repositories about investing, finance and Web3. What is scarce is the usable part. Strategy code expires, ticker calls have a half-life of weeks, dead links outnumber live ones. What survives is the mechanism: where the money comes from, where the risk sits, how to check the claim.

This handbook takes dozens of sources apart and reorders them into eight chapters along the actual decision path. Each one ends in something you can use: six red flags in financial statements, four valuation rulers, the five-part anatomy of a scam, four holes in a backtest, four sources of DeFi yield, six due-diligence questions. Anything that could not explain where the return comes from stayed out, however professional it looked.

The books were not taken wholesale either. All 21 entries say what problem the book solves, and where it stops being useful.

**Three rules:**

- Keep the mechanism, drop the conclusion. Conclusions expire.
- Give standards, not tickers. A list makes the decision for you.
- Only what can be verified. What cannot be verified is labelled as such.

> Decide that you will buy the same thing for years before deciding what it is.

### Value investing

> Margin of safety, circle of competence, cycles. The foundation of everything here.

- [The Intelligent Investor](books/chinese-canon/《聪明的投资者》%28美%29本杰明·格雷厄姆.pdf) - Benjamin Graham · 17.7 MB · PDF. Margin of safety and Mr Market. The grammar of value investing, concentrated in chapters 8 and 20.
- [Security Analysis](books/chinese-canon/《证券分析》本杰明•格雷厄姆.pdf) - Benjamin Graham & David Dodd · 2.3 MB · PDF. Harder than The Intelligent Investor. Use it as a reference, not a read-through.
- [Poor Charlie's Almanack](books/chinese-canon/《穷查理宝典》.pdf) - Peter D. Kaufman (ed.) · 3.7 MB · PDF. Mental models and inversion. The speeches carry it; the rest is arrangement.
- [Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits](books/chinese-canon/《怎样选择成长股》.pdf) - Philip Fisher · 5.9 MB · PDF. The fifteen points are a usable question list. The original growth-investing framework.
- [Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (full translation)](books/chinese-canon/《怎样选择成长股最新全译版》.pdf) - Philip Fisher · 23.7 MB · PDF. A fuller translation of the same book. Pick one of the two.
- [The Simplest Thing in Investing](books/chinese-canon/《投资中最简单的事》.pdf) - Qiu Guolu · 5.5 MB · PDF. The most local cases. Industry structure and pricing power, closer to A-shares than any translation.
- [The Most Important Thing](books/chinese-canon/《投资最重要的事》.pdf) - Howard Marks · 18.5 MB · PDF. Cycles, risk and second-level thinking in memo form. Worth rereading.

### Financial statements

> The goal is to rule companies out, not to become an accountant.

- [Financial Statements in One Book](books/chinese-canon/《一本书读懂财报（全新修订版）》.pdf) - Xiao Xing · 2.5 MB · PDF. The fastest Chinese introduction to statements. You finish able to read the three together.

### Index funds

> The default answer once you admit you cannot pick single stocks.

- [The Index Fund Guide](books/chinese-canon/《指数基金投资指南》.pdf) - Yinhang Luosiding · 11.6 MB · PDF. Practical index-fund valuation and averaging. Tool-like, though written a while ago.

### Asset allocation

> It sets most of your long-run outcome, more than stock picking does.

- [Stocks for the Long Run (5th ed.)](books/chinese-canon/《股市长线法宝（原书第5版）》.pdf) - Jeremy Siegel · 18.1 MB · PDF. Two centuries of data on where long-run return comes from. A cure for short-term anxiety.
- [The Art of Asset Allocation](books/chinese-canon/《资产配置的艺术%28高清%29》.pdf) - David Darst · 25.4 MB · PDF. A textbook survey of asset allocation, chart-heavy, good as a manual.

### Market history

> Long-run data is the cheapest cure for short-run panic.

- [Everybody Ought to Be Rich (1929)](books/on-regular-investing/EverybodyOughtToBeRich-1929.pdf) - John J. Raskob · 846 KB · EN PDF. The 1929 essay where the averaging argument starts. Three pages.
- [Reminiscences of a Stock Operator](books/chinese-canon/《股票作手回忆录》.pdf) - Edwin Lefèvre · 3.3 MB · PDF. The classic narrative of a trader's psychology. Read it as a story, not a method.

### Psychology

> Knowing a bias exists is more realistic than trying to defeat it.

- [The Crowd: A Study of the Popular Mind](books/chinese-canon/《乌合之众》古斯塔夫·勒庞.pdf) - Gustave Le Bon · 792 KB · PDF. An 1895 essay of observation. Take the intuition, drop the claims. It is not research.

### Money basics

> For complete beginners, and for children.

- [Rich Dad Poor Dad](books/chinese-canon/《富爸爸,穷爸爸》.pdf) - Robert Kiyosaki · 728 KB · PDF. An entry point to cash-flow thinking. One idea worth keeping, the tactics are dated.
- [Kira and a Dog Named Money](books/chinese-canon/《小狗钱钱》.pdf) - Bodo Schäfer · 3.8 MB · PDF. For children or complete beginners. Saving, goals and delayed gratification.
- [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant](books/chinese-canon/《纳瓦尔宝典》.pdf) - Eric Jorgenson · 3.9 MB · PDF. On wealth and happiness. Conceptual, no executable method.
- [The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (alt. edition)](books/chinese-canon/《纳瓦尔宝典财富和幸福指南》.pdf) - Eric Jorgenson · 3.0 MB · PDF. Another edition of the same book. Highly duplicative, pick one.

## FAQ

**Why is there no strategy code?** Code expires, and a good backtest is separated from live profit by costs, slippage and capacity. Chapter 06 gives the boundaries, so a beautiful curve tells you what to ask.

**Why no tickers?** Asset-level information has a half-life of weeks. A framework outlasts it.

**Why no shelf books for chapters 06 and 07?** Chinese-language quant and Web3 writing is mostly strategy collections and project promotion. A forced entry is worse than none.

**Can the books be reposted or used commercially?** No. Copyright stays with the authors and publishers; this repository is a local archive. See [NOTICE.md](NOTICE.md).

**Why was the repository roster removed?** A table produces no understanding; the reader still opens every row. This version rewrites the content and keeps provenance only in the copyright notice.

**Will it be updated?** Chapters are stable; the shelf grows as needed. The page and both READMEs come from one manifest, so they cannot drift apart.

## Editorial choices

**Only mechanisms survive.** Anything that could not explain its source of return, could not be falsified, or rested on "trust me" stayed out. What remains can be checked: the six red flags against a real report, the six questions against a real protocol.

**What was cut.** Strategy code and signals, because they expire. Ticker calls, because they rot. Motivational filler, because it builds no judgment. The popular books were not taken wholesale: *The Crowd* is an 1895 essay, not research; *Rich Dad Poor Dad* keeps one durable idea about cash flow and its tactics are dated; *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* is philosophy without method.

**The page is the same content read differently.** `index.html` adds a chapter sidebar, full-text search and per-format downloads, with a language switch. This README is its index.
