## 一句话

投资失败多数不是因为不懂金融，是因为管不住自己，以及相信了不该信的人。

## 六个会反复出现的偏差

- **损失厌恶**。亏一百的痛苦大约是赚一百快乐的两倍，于是死拿亏损股，卖掉赚钱股。
- **确认偏误**。买入之后只看利好，把利空解释成"短期波动"。
- **锚定**。把买入价当成本线，用"回本就走"代替重新判断。
- **近期偏好**。用最近三个月的走势推断未来三年。
- **羊群效应**。看到群里都在赚，怕错过，于是在最热的时候进场。
- **后见之明**。事后觉得"早就该看出来"，于是高估自己的判断力，下一次下更重的注。

对抗方法不是意志力，是流程。决策前写下理由与证伪条件，定期回看。写在纸上，骗不了自己。

## 骗局的五个通用结构

不管包装成什么，骗局都要解决同一个问题：让人相信高收益没有风险。

1. **收益来源说不清**。问"钱从哪来"，回答是"量化套利""搬砖""AI 策略"这类无法验证的词。
2. **有人在承担你的收益**。庞氏结构里，早期收益来自后来者的本金，所以必须不断拉新人。
3. **为什么找你**。真有稳定高收益的策略，资金成本远低于向你募资的成本。
4. **退出被设计成困难**。提现要排队、要手续费、要升级会员、要拉人头解锁。
5. **权威与稀缺**。"老师""内部渠道""名额只剩三个""今天最后一天"。

## 常见话术的翻译

- "保本高息" → 要么不保本，要么不是高息，要么两者都不是。
- "内幕消息" → 真内幕不会免费给你，免费给你的通常是出货信号。
- "老师带单，稳赚" → 老师的收入来自你的手续费或你的亏损。
- "先小赚几次建立信任" → 这是标准流程，不是运气。
- "国家支持的新项目" → 查牌照，别查口号。

## 关于杠杆

杠杆不改变胜率，只改变破产速度。百分之五十的跌幅，两倍杠杆就归零，而市场出现百分之五十的跌幅在历史上并不罕见。

用杠杆的唯一合理场景，是现金流确定覆盖利息且期限匹配。用杠杆抄底，是把自己的生死交给市场情绪。

## 对畅销书的一点保留

《乌合之众》成书于 1895 年，是观察随笔，不是实验研究，里面很多论断今天站不住。它有用，是因为它让你意识到群体里人的判断力会下降，而不是因为它科学。

《富爸爸穷爸爸》的核心是"资产产生现金流、负债消耗现金流"，这一句值得记住。但书里的具体操作建议（尤其是房产与杠杆部分）带有强烈的时代与国别局限，照抄会出事。

读书要区分三种内容：可验证的机制、作者的经验、作者的观点。只有第一种能直接拿来用。

最近一次亏损，是因为判断错了，还是因为情绪、杠杆、或者信了别人？

## In one sentence

Most investment failure is not a finance problem. It is self-control, plus trusting the wrong person.

## Six biases that keep returning

- **Loss aversion.** A loss hurts roughly twice as much as an equal gain feels good, so losers get held and winners get sold.
- **Confirmation bias.** After buying, only good news registers and bad news becomes "short-term noise".
- **Anchoring.** The purchase price becomes the reference, and "sell when I break even" replaces fresh analysis.
- **Recency.** Three months of price action becomes a three-year forecast.
- **Herding.** Everyone in the group chat is making money, so you buy at the hottest point.
- **Hindsight.** "I should have seen it" inflates confidence, and the next bet is larger.

Willpower is not the countermeasure. Process is. Write the thesis and the disconfirming condition before buying, then reread it on schedule. On paper, you cannot fool yourself.

## The five-part structure of every scam

Whatever the wrapping, a scam must solve one problem: make people believe high return comes without risk.

1. **The source of return is unverifiable.** Ask where the money comes from and hear "quant arbitrage", "AI strategy", "market making". Words that cannot be checked.
2. **Someone else funds your return.** In a Ponzi structure early payouts come from later deposits, so new money must keep arriving.
3. **Why you.** A genuinely stable high-return strategy would raise money far cheaper than pitching strangers.
4. **Exit is engineered to be hard.** Withdrawal queues, fees, membership upgrades, referral unlocks.
5. **Authority and scarcity.** "The teacher", "internal channel", "three slots left", "today only".

## Translating the pitch

- "Guaranteed principal, high yield" means it is not guaranteed, not high, or neither.
- "Inside information" is never free. Free tips are distribution, not generosity.
- "Follow the teacher, steady profit" means the teacher earns from your commissions or your losses.
- "Let them win small first" is a script, not luck.
- "State-backed new project" means check the licence, not the slogan.

## On leverage

Leverage does not change the odds, it changes the speed of ruin. A fifty percent decline wipes out two-times leverage, and fifty percent declines are not rare.

The only defensible use is when cash flow reliably covers the interest and the maturity matches. Using leverage to buy a dip hands your survival to other people's mood.

## A caveat on the bestsellers

*The Crowd* was published in 1895. It is an essay of observation, not experimental research, and much of it does not survive modern scrutiny. It is useful because it makes you notice that judgment degrades in groups, not because it is science.

*Rich Dad Poor Dad* has one durable idea: assets produce cash flow, liabilities consume it. Its specific advice, especially on property and leverage, is tightly bound to a particular market and era. Copying it directly is dangerous.

Separate three things in any book: mechanisms you can verify, the author's experience, and the author's opinions. Only the first transfers.
