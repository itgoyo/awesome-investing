## 一句话

区块链解决的是"没有中间人时如何达成状态共识"，代价是性能与成本。加密资产是另一件事，两者要分开看。

## 技术层：它到底解决了什么

传统系统里，你转账依赖银行记账，依赖法院执行。区块链把这本账交给一群互不信任的节点共同维护，规则写在代码里，改规则需要多数同意。

代价很明确：慢、贵、出错不可撤销。所以它适合的场景是"跨主体、无信任基础、需要公开可验证"，不适合高频小额支付。

**私钥就是所有权**。没有客服，没有找回密码。丢私钥等于丢资产，这是设计特性，不是缺陷。

**智能合约是代码即法律**。代码有漏洞，钱就没了，没有撤销按钮。授权（approve）是最大的攻击面，无限授权等于把钱包钥匙交给合约。

## 经济层：代币的收益从哪来

判断一个代币项目，只需要回答一个问题：它的现金流从谁的口袋来？

- **协议收入**：有人真的为服务付费，费用分给代币持有者。这类极少。
- **增发与激励**：用新发的代币补贴用户，本质是补贴，补贴停了，行为就停。
- **后来者的钱**：没有收入，靠新买家接盘。这是多数项目的真相。

代币分配表比白皮书重要。解锁时间表、团队份额、早期投资者成本，这三个数字决定了谁会持续卖。

## DeFi 的收益拆解

- **借贷利差**：存款人拿利息，借款人付利息，协议抽成。收益来自真实需求，但利率随行情剧烈波动。
- **做市手续费**：为交易对提供流动性赚手续费，同时承担无常损失。手续费能不能覆盖无常损失，取决于波动率。
- **激励补贴**：协议发币补贴流动性，年化很高。补贴一旦停止，收益率归零。
- **循环抵押**：反复抵押借出再抵押，放大杠杆。清算线一旦触发，连环爆仓。
 
看到两位数年化，先问属于上面哪一类。第三类和第四类不是收益，是风险溢价。

## 五类高频骗局

- **假空投**。诱导你签名授权，签完钱包被清空。
- **貔貅盘**。能买不能卖，合约里写死了。
- **拉高出货**。社群喊单、KOL 站台，价格拉高后团队砸盘。
- **假客服**。主动私聊，让你"验证钱包"，实为钓鱼。
- **质押跑路**。高息质押，本质是资金盘，跑路时间写在代码或人性里。

## 尽调清单

合约是否开源并经过独立审计？流动性是否锁定、锁多久？团队是否实名、过往项目是否还在？代币分配与解锁表？有没有真实的收入，收入来自谁？退出通道是否顺畅，深度够不够？

六问里有三个答不上来，就不该投。这不是保守，是基本盘。

## In one sentence

A blockchain solves state consensus without a trusted intermediary, at the cost of throughput and fees. Crypto assets are a separate question. Keep the two apart.

## The technical layer

In a normal system, a transfer depends on a bank's ledger and on courts to enforce. A blockchain hands that ledger to mutually distrusting nodes, with rules in code and changes requiring majority agreement.

The costs are explicit: slow, expensive, irreversible. So it fits cases that are cross-party, have no trust baseline, and need public verifiability. It does not fit high-frequency small payments.

**The private key is the ownership.** No support desk, no password reset. Losing the key is losing the asset. That is the design, not a bug.

**A smart contract is code as law.** A bug means the money is gone, with no undo. Approval is the largest attack surface. An infinite approval hands the contract your keys.

## The economic layer

To judge a token, answer one question. Whose pocket does its cash flow come from?

- **Protocol revenue.** Someone actually pays for a service and the fee flows to holders. Rare.
- **Emissions.** Users are paid with newly issued tokens. That is a subsidy. When it stops, the behaviour stops.
- **Later buyers.** No revenue, price sustained by new entrants. This is the majority case.

The allocation table matters more than the whitepaper. Unlock schedule, team share, early investor cost. Those three numbers decide who keeps selling.

## Decomposing DeFi yield

- **Lending spread.** Depositors earn, borrowers pay, the protocol takes a cut. Real demand, but rates swing violently.
- **Market-making fees.** Earn fees providing liquidity, carry impermanent loss. Whether fees cover the loss depends on volatility.
- **Incentives.** The protocol pays you in its own token for liquidity. High APY, zero when emissions stop.
- **Recursive collateral.** Deposit, borrow, deposit again. Leverage on leverage, with liquidation cascades.

When you see double-digit APY, classify it. The third and fourth categories are not yield, they are risk premia.

## Five recurring scams

- **Fake airdrops.** A signature request that drains the wallet once approved.
- **Honeypots.** Buying works, selling is disabled in the contract.
- **Pump and dump.** Group calls and paid promoters lift the price while the team distributes.
- **Fake support.** A direct message asking you to "validate your wallet". Phishing.
- **Staking exits.** High-yield staking that is a deposit scheme. The exit date lives in the code or in the operator's patience.

## Due diligence checklist

Is the contract open source and independently audited? Is liquidity locked, and for how long? Is the team identifiable and are their prior projects alive? What is the allocation and unlock schedule? Is there real revenue, and who pays it? Can you exit, and is there depth?

Three unanswered questions out of six means no position. That is not conservatism, it is the floor.

## Self-check

Can you say whose payment produces this token's return, rather than whose purchase?
